How to use this list

Read the two number columns together. A high request count means the trope is wanted; a high underserved figure means the shelf has not caught up. Romance book tropes where both run high — slow burn, forced proximity, enemies-to-lovers, dark romance — are open lanes, not crowded ones. That pairing is the whole point of tracking demand rather than trusting a static list.

Tropes rarely travel alone

Most published romance stacks three to five of these tropes at once — enemies-to-lovers plus forced proximity plus slow burn, say. The romance tropes list tells you what each ingredient is worth on its own; a Tropesmith Map scores your exact combination against live demand and supply so you can see whether your stack lands in an open lane or a crowded one.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most popular romance tropes?

By live reader demand, the most-requested romance book tropes Tropesmith tracks are found family, slow burn, forced proximity, enemies-to-lovers and dark romance. The full romance tropes list above ranks all 25 by request count.

What is a romance trope?

A romance trope is a recognisable pattern readers seek out on purpose — a relationship dynamic (enemies-to-lovers, grumpy-sunshine), a setup (forced proximity, marriage of convenience) or a pacing choice (slow burn). Readers use tropes to find the exact reading experience they want.

How many romance tropes are there?

There is no fixed number, and any site quoting one is guessing. Tropesmith's trope taxonomy holds 2,059 named tropes, and 2,015 of them carry live reader demand in our data. This romance tropes list focuses on the 25 with the highest live reader demand, which is what matters most when you are positioning a book.

Compared with the tools authors usually reach for. Publisher Rocket reports Amazon keyword and category data, KDSpy reads bestseller pages for a quick niche verdict, BookBeam tracks how published books perform over time, and K-lytics writes up a genre as of its report date. All four describe the shelf that already exists. None of them counts what readers asked for and did not get — which is the only number on this page.
