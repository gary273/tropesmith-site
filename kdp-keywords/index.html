KDP Keywords: How to Choose Your Seven

Updated 17 October 2026 · KDP rules are from Amazon KDP’s own help page · Written by Tropesmith, which sells a Map that touches on keyword choice, so read the tool comparison with that in mind

KDP keywords are the up to seven keywords or short phrases you enter in Amazon KDP’s book details to tell Amazon which searches your book should appear in. Seven is a ceiling, not a target. Choose words a reader would actually type that your book truly delivers, and check each one by searching it on Amazon before you publish.

How many KDP keywords do you get, and can you change them?

You get up to seven keywords or short phrases, and you can change them as often as you like. KDP’s help says to use up to seven and to keep an eye on the character limit in the text field. To edit them, go to your Bookshelf, click the ellipsis button next to your book, choose Edit book details for your book format, add or edit your keywords under the Keywords section, click Save and Continue, and on the Pricing page click Publish.

KDP’s help also says: “Single words work better than phrases, and specific words work better than general ones.” Its example is a phrase typed inside quotation marks: with “complex suspenseful whodunit” in quotation marks, only people who type all of those words will find your book, whereas without them customers can search for any of those words. So KDP lists quotation marks in search terms among the things to avoid.

The same help page lists more keywords to avoid, and says the list is not exhaustive and that all keywords must comply with KDP’s Terms and Conditions:

  • Information covered elsewhere in your book’s metadata, such as your title and contributors, and words already in your book categories.
  • Subjective claims about quality, such as “best novel ever”.
  • Time-sensitive statements, such as “new”, “on sale” and “available now”.
  • Information common to most items in the category, such as “book”, and spelling errors.
  • Variants of spacing, punctuation, capitalization and pluralization.
  • Amazon program names such as “Kindle Unlimited” or “KDP Select”, and HTML tags.
  • Anything misrepresentative, such as the name of an author not associated with your book, and brands you do not own or are not authorized to use.

How do I choose my seven KDP keywords?

Start from the book and work out toward the search, not the other way round:

  1. Start from what the book delivers. KDP’s advice is to think like a reader and imagine how you would search as a customer. Its list of useful keyword types is setting, character types, character roles, plot themes and story tone.
  2. Collect candidates for free. Amazon’s search bar suggests phrases as you type, and KDP’s help points to the suggestions in the Search drop-down. Trope names are the vocabulary many fiction readers use, such as enemies to lovers, slow burn or forced proximity.
  3. Search each candidate on Amazon before you publish. KDP’s help says to search using the keywords you are considering and, if you get irrelevant or unsatisfying results, to make some changes. A check of our own: open the first few books that come up, and if they do not look like yours, drop the keyword.
  4. Put the words in the order a customer would type them. KDP’s own example: customers search for “military science fiction” but probably not for “fiction science military”.
  5. Cover different angles, not seven versions of one idea. This one is our advice rather than KDP’s: mix a trope, a setting, a character type and a tone, and skip anything already in your title or categories.
  6. Revisit after launch. KDP says you can change keywords and descriptions as often as you like, so treat what you enter as something to tune.

What are the best KDP keywords for romance?

There is no universal list, but good romance keywords describe what your book truly delivers in the words readers use. In KDP’s terms, a trope such as second chance is usually a plot theme, “small town” is a setting, “single dad” is a character type (KDP’s own example) and “feel-good” is a story tone (also KDP’s example). Because KDP says specific words work better than general ones, keep each entry short and specific rather than stuffing in a long phrase. Frequently asked questions

What are KDP keywords?

KDP keywords are the up to seven keywords or short phrases you enter in Amazon KDP’s book details to help Amazon decide which searches your book should appear in. They sit alongside your title, categories and description.

How many keywords can you use on KDP?

Up to seven keywords or short phrases, according to KDP’s help page. KDP also says that quotation marks in search terms are among the things to avoid, and that single words work better than phrases and specific words work better than general ones.

How do I find the best KDP keywords for my book?

Start from what your book truly delivers, collect candidate words from Amazon’s search bar and from the trope names readers use, then search each candidate on Amazon before you publish. If the results do not look like your book, drop it.

Can I change my KDP keywords after publishing?

Yes. In KDP, go to your Bookshelf, choose Edit book details for the book, change the Keywords section, save, and publish again. KDP’s help says you can change keywords and descriptions as often as you like.

Does Tropesmith show keyword search volume?

No. A Map does not show Amazon search volumes. It includes a relative Google search-interest panel and a Titles and search terms section that lists search terms that fit your book and the terms it removed, with reasons. Check each term against KDP’s list of keywords to avoid before you use it.

What is the best tool for KDP keywords?

It depends on the job. Amazon’s search bar is free. Publisher Rocket lists a keyword search for $199 one-time on its own site, as of 17 October 2026. A Tropesmith Map costs $15 and lists search terms that fit your book and the terms it removed, without search volumes.
