KDSpy Alternative: What to Use Instead, and When

Updated 17 October 2026 · Written by Tropesmith, which competes with some tools on this page, so read it with that in mind · Prices and features are as listed on kdspy.com, publisherrocket.com and k-lytics.com on 17 October 2026

The closest KDSpy alternative for the same job is Publisher Rocket, which also researches Amazon keywords and categories. Tropesmith is not a like-for-like swap: it shows which tropes readers want in your romance subgenre, and it costs $15 per Map. Every price below is as listed on the vendor's own site on 17 October 2026.

What does KDSpy do, and what does it cost?

KDSpy, sold as KDSPY Pro, is a browser-based research tool for Amazon, and on kdspy.com it was listed at $79 as a one-time payment on 17 October 2026, against a regular price of $197, with no monthly fee. Per its own page, it does the following:

  • Breaks down any of the 25,000+ Kindle, book or audiobook categories on Amazon in one click.
  • Estimates how many copies a book needs to sell in 24 hours to rank #1 to #20 in a category.
  • Suggests keywords from any keyword search, with revenue potential and competitor counts, exportable for Amazon ads.
  • Tracks the sales rank and estimated revenue of any book over 30 days.
  • Uses country-specific data from Amazon stores in more than a dozen countries, with Poland marked as just added.

kdspy.com also says it will not work on iPads, tablets or smartphones, and it offers a 60-day money-back guarantee. kdspy.com describes some figures, such as profit and competitors' monthly revenue, as estimated. The $79 price is shown as a promotion, so check the vendor before you buy.

Which tool fits which job?

Pick by the question you are trying to answer, because the tools above answer different ones.

The questionWhere to look
Is this Amazon category winnable, and what does the top of it earn?KDSpy lists category analysis with an estimated profit and the sales needed to rank. Publisher Rocket lists category search and a competition analyzer that shows rivals' sales and rankings.
Which Amazon keywords should I use, and which should I bid on?Publisher Rocket lists a keyword search and an Amazon ads keyword generator. KDSpy lists keyword suggestions you can export for ads.
How is a whole Kindle category doing month to month?K-lytics lists monthly market updates.
Which tropes and story angle should I write for my romance subgenre?A Tropesmith Map. Trope-level reader demand is not among the features listed on the KDSpy or Publisher Rocket home pages.

Is there a free KDSpy alternative?

Partly: Amazon's own search bar is free, and KDP's help page tells authors to search Amazon with the keywords they are considering before they publish. Tropesmith's free tools cover other pieces, and none of them tracks a book's sales or shows Amazon search volume:

  • Category & Rank Checker: every category a book ranks in, and where it sits in each.
  • Lane Score: whether a subgenre is worth writing in, by opportunity score, typical price and the heat level and length readers expect.
  • Trope Demand Checker: pick a genre and a trope to see counted mentions, share of the genre and whether it is rising or cooling.

Does Tropesmith replace KDSpy?

No. Tropesmith has no browser extension, does not track individual books' sales and does not show Amazon search volume, though a Map does include lane-level earnings estimates. It is meant to be used before or alongside a niche-research tool, because it answers an earlier question: which story should I write? Maps are built for romance and adjacent lanes, from your subgenre, heat level, format and length, and a Map includes:

  • A go or no-go opportunity score for your subgenre.
  • A ranked trope stack with demand against supply, and the gaps worth owning.
  • Comp titles, with notes on what to borrow and what to avoid.
  • A sample blurb, hook lines, cover direction, and search terms that fit your book.
  • The categories books like yours sit in, a price plan and a step-by-step plan.

A Map is research, not a writing tool: the book is still yours to write. You can read two complete sample Maps before you spend anything.
