Publisher Rocket vs BookBeam

Written by Tropesmith, which competes with the tool on this page — read it with that in mind · Rival details: publisherrocket.com and bookbeam.io

The short answer: the real difference is snapshot versus tracking. Publisher Rocket is typically a one-time purchase that answers keyword and category questions when you sit down with it. BookBeam is a subscription whose distinguishing feature is watching books and categories over time. Research once per book and never look again: Rocket. Running launches and ads and needing to see movement: BookBeam. Pricing for both is on their own sites and we do not track it. Both describe published books — which is the thing neither is built to see past.

Rocket: the one-off answer

Keyword research with competition scoring, category research at a granularity Amazon's browse tree hides, ads keyword lists. You buy it once and use it per book. There is no ongoing cost and no ongoing signal.

BookBeam: the moving picture

Rank curves over time, competitor launches as they unfold, review movement, your own title's decay after a promo. This is information a snapshot structurally cannot give you, and it is the reason to pay a subscription rather than buy once.

What both share, and what both miss

Both are anchored to books that exist. One photographs the shelf, the other films it. Tropesmith adds demand signals from reviews and BookTok comments, which have no rank to track and no keyword to score.

What Tropesmith does instead

Here is the distinction that matters, and it is not a slight on anyone's tool. Keyword and category research answers how do I get this book found. It assumes the book exists. Tropesmith answers a question that comes earlier and that no keyword tool attempts: which tropes readers ask for and praise, and how many demand signals each one has. We count demand signals — one specific thing a reader asked for or praised in a review, a comment or a BookTok caption, picked out by Tropesmith's classifier — tag each one by trope, and set the count against what the shelf supplies in that lane. Tropesmith reads a registry of 520,000+ books, 4,320,000+ Goodreads reviews, 968,000+ book–trope tags across 116,000+ fully tagged titles, and 2,620,000+ demand signals from reviews, comments and posts, scored across 140+ demand lanes — plus a scan of 730+ BookTok hashtags (135,000+ videos logged, 106,200+ demand signals from BookTok comments and captions).

What Tropesmith is not. It is not a keyword tool: it will not give you Amazon search volumes or seven backend keyword strings. It does not estimate anyone's sales or revenue. It does not build or bid your ads. If those are the jobs in front of you, the tool on this page does them and Tropesmith does not replace it. Plenty of authors run both, and that is the honest recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Publisher Rocket or BookBeam - which should I buy?

Rocket if your research is a one-off per book and you want depth on keywords and categories without an ongoing bill. BookBeam if you are running launches or ads and need to see rank and reviews move over time, which a snapshot tool cannot show you at all.

Is BookBeam better than Publisher Rocket?

Not better, different. BookBeam's tracking is something Rocket does not do; Rocket's keyword competition depth and one-time cost are things BookBeam's subscription does not match. Pick on whether your question is 'what should this listing say' or 'what is happening over time'.

Do I need both?

Most authors do not. The overlap on keyword and category research is substantial; the non-overlap is tracking, which you either need or do not.

What do Publisher Rocket and BookBeam both miss?

Both are anchored to published titles - keywords they rank for, charts they sit on, curves they trace. Tropesmith adds demand signals from reviews, comments and posts: 2,620,000+ of them across 140+ demand lanes, each trope carrying the share tagged underserved or novel by Tropesmith's classifier.

Can I use a keyword tool and Tropesmith together?

Yes, and that is the sensible setup. Tropesmith decides what to write and which trope stack to build on; Rocket or BookBeam handle the listing and the tracking once it exists.
