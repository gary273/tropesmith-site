K-lytics Review: What the Reports Cover, and What They Do Not

Updated 21 August 2026 · Written by Tropesmith, which competes with the tool on this page — read it with that in mind · Rival details: k-lytics.com

The short version: K-lytics genre reports are analyst-written studies of a book category — how crowded it is, what the top of it charges, how many reviews the leaders carry, which subcategories are growing. They are a real shortcut into an unfamiliar genre. The one thing to check before you buy is the date on the report for your genre, because the value decays from publication day and it decays faster in fast-moving romance subgenres than in stable ones. We build the competing kind of data, so read this knowing that — and note that the honest verdict below is that a report and live demand data answer different questions.

What the reports do well

Synthesis. Raw category numbers are easy to gather and hard to interpret, and a written report hands you the interpretation: this subcategory is saturated, that one is thin, this price band is where the reads are. If you are moving into a genre you do not read yourself, that is worth paying for, and it is faster than assembling the same picture by hand.

Where they date

A report is fixed at its publication date. Category composition, pricing norms and which subgenres are rising all move — sometimes inside a single season, as anyone who watched romantasy reshape the romance charts can attest. Before buying, check when the report covering your genre was last refreshed, and treat anything more than a few months old as direction rather than fact.

The resolution question

Reports work at category level: how Regency Romance behaves. The decision an author is usually making sits one level down, at trope level: which premise inside that category readers keep asking for and not getting. That is not a flaw in the reports, it is a different unit of analysis — and it is the unit Tropesmith works in.

What Tropesmith does instead

Here is the distinction that matters, and it is not a slight on anyone's tool. Keyword and category research answers how do I get this book found. It assumes the book exists. Tropesmith answers a question that comes earlier and that no keyword tool attempts: which story do readers keep asking for and not getting. We count individual reader requests — a real person, in a review or a comment or a BookTok video, describing a book they want and cannot find — classify each one by trope, and score it against what the shelf actually supplies in that lane. Tropesmith reads a registry of 310,000+ books, 3,140,000+ Goodreads reviews, 412,000+ book–trope tags across 49,700+ fully tagged titles, and 856,004 classified reader requests, scored across 138 demand lanes — plus an hourly scan of 620+ BookTok hashtags (95,800+ videos logged, 34,250 reader requests read from BookTok).

What Tropesmith is not. It is not a keyword tool: it will not give you Amazon search volumes or seven backend keyword strings. It does not estimate anyone's sales or revenue. It does not build or bid your ads. If those are the jobs in front of you, the tool on this page does them and Tropesmith does not replace it. Plenty of authors run both, and that is the honest recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Is K-lytics worth it?

If you are entering a genre you do not know and want the landscape summarised, a recently updated report is a genuine shortcut. Check the refresh date for your specific genre first - that is the single variable that decides whether it is worth it. Pricing is on the vendor's site and we do not track it.

What does a K-lytics report contain?

Broadly: how large and competitive a book category is, what the books in it charge, how many reviews the leaders carry, and which subcategories are growing or shrinking, with an analyst's reading of what that means. Coverage varies by genre - check the sample or table of contents for the specific report before buying.

How current is K-lytics data?

As current as the report's publication date, which is the structural limit of any report. Genres move; romance subgenres move fastest. Always look at when the report for your genre was last refreshed rather than when the catalogue page was last touched.

What is the alternative to a genre report?

Live data you query yourself. Tropesmith scores 138 demand lanes from 856,004 classified reader requests, and publishes the lane snapshots and trope demand pages free on this site with the count date on every figure. It gives you no written analysis - that is the trade.

Is this review impartial?

No, and pretending otherwise would be worse. We build a competing kind of data and we would rather you knew that while reading. What we have tried to do is be accurate about what the reports genuinely do well - synthesis and speed into an unfamiliar genre - and specific about the one real limitation, which is that a fixed-date report drifts.
