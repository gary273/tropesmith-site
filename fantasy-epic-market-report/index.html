How to read entrenchment

Entrenchment is the inverse of chart turnover: 67.0% means that share of the top-100 titles on the snapshot date were already on the chart at the comparison scrape at least 14 days earlier. High entrenchment lanes reward a strong, differentiated hook — low entrenchment lanes reward speed to market.

What the price range means for positioning

The $3.99–$22.17 spread around a $11.99 typical price isn't just a pricing footnote — it maps roughly to how a title gets positioned. Books landing near the floor tend to be debuts, backlist deep cuts, or titles competing mainly on volume and discoverability rather than brand. Books near the premium end are usually backed by either an established readership or a hook distinctive enough to justify asking readers to pay above the lane's norm. This economics read (confidence: live-amazon, data grade bankable) is refreshed weekly, so treat any single snapshot as a point-in-time read rather than a fixed rule.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an epic fantasy book typically sell for?

Tropesmith's economics read puts the typical price at $11.99, with a floor of $3.99 and a premium tier around $22.17 (confidence: live-amazon).

Is epic fantasy an oversaturated lane?

Tropesmith has linked 4,321 titles to this lane through Amazon category pages since it began tracking it in July 2026 (a count that grows as coverage grows). The top-100 chart shows 67.0% entrenchment: 33.0% of the top-100 were not on the chart at the comparison scrape at least 14 days earlier. High entrenchment means incumbents hold their chart spots; it doesn't mean new entrants can't break in, only that they're competing against a stable chart rather than a churning one.
