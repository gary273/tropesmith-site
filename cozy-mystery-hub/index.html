A signal "mentions" a trope when it is one of the signal's main or secondary tags. What "Cozy Mystery" promises readers

Cozy mystery readers want a low-violence puzzle, an amateur sleuth with a day job that gives them cover to investigate, and a community small enough that the killer is someone the reader has already met. The genre runs on voice and setting as much as plot — a bakery, a bookshop, a small town with too many secrets — which is why series identity matters more here than almost anywhere else in mystery.

53,100 demand signals mention it, and 32% carry the underserved or novel tag. Tropesmith's registry of trope-tagged books holds 3 titles for it; the registry is deepest in romance, so that count reflects tagging coverage and says little about how crowded the real cozy shelf is. What readers reject in mystery

Demand tells you what readers want written. Rejection tells you how they'll punish it if you get the execution wrong. Tropesmith mines 1–2★ Goodreads reviews for the specific reason a reader gave up, then classifies it against a fixed list of dealbreaker patterns:

Slow / dragging pace — named 10,435 times in 1–2★ Goodreads reviews, summed over the genre's lanes (a review can count more than once); 61% of those mentions sit in reviews the classifier marked as a DNF.
Boring / failed to hold interest — named 5,719 times in 1–2★ Goodreads reviews, summed over the genre's lanes (a review can count more than once); 70% of those mentions sit in reviews the classifier marked as a DNF.
Thin character development — named 5,700 times in 1–2★ Goodreads reviews, summed over the genre's lanes (a review can count more than once); 59% of those mentions sit in reviews the classifier marked as a DNF.

How to use this before you draft

Read the numbers together, not separately. 53,100 demand signals tell you how often readers mention cozy mystery in the reviews, comments and posts Tropesmith has read; 32% tagged underserved or novel tells you how often the classifier tagged those mentions underserved or novel; the registry count tells you how many books Tropesmith has tagged with it. None of them says whether a book that delivers the trope will sell, and none shows what is on the whole shelf. A high tagged share is a prompt to check comp titles for the specific angles readers name; a low one means the classifier tagged fewer of the mentions that way. Pair any of it with the rejection data above: readers punish weak execution of a trope they want just as fast as they ignore one they don't.
