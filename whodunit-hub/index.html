What "Whodunit" promises readers

Whodunit is the classic-form mystery promise: a closed set of suspects, a fair-play puzzle, and a solution the reader could theoretically have reached first if they'd paid close enough attention. It's the structural backbone the rest of mystery riffs on — cozy softens it, thriller speeds it up, noir darkens it — but the pure form still has its own dedicated audience.

Demand for it runs steady rather than spiky, which is typical of a structural trope versus a trend-driven one. Tropesmith classifies it "balanced": meaningful tagged supply already exists, so the opportunity here is less "nobody's writing this" and more finding the specific angle — setting, sleuth archetype, era — that isn't already crowded.

How to use this before you draft

Read the three numbers above together, not separately. 27,522 requests tells you the trope is wanted at real scale; 100% underserved tells you how much of that want has no matching tagged supply yet; and the 13% recent-request share tells you whether that gap is closing or holding steady right now. A high request count with low underserved means the trope is proven but the shelf has largely caught up — the opportunity is in a specific unclaimed angle, not the trope's bare existence. A high underserved figure means more room, but pair it with the rejection data above: readers punish weak execution of a trope they want just as fast as they ignore one they don't.
