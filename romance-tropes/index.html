Romance Tropes, Ranked by Demand Signals

Per-trope counts as of 7 October 2026 · Source: Tropesmith demand-signal database

Most romance tropes lists are opinion. This one is a count. Tropesmith has analyzed 2,610,000+ demand signals — specific things readers asked for or praised in reviews, comments and posts — and tags each one with the tropes it mentions, where it names any. 39% are tagged underserved or novel by Tropesmith's classifier; 95.6% come from Goodreads and Amazon reviews, 4.1% from BookTok comments and captions and 0.3% from Reddit posts. That lets us rank romance tropes by how many demand signals mention them, and show what share of each trope's signals carry that tag.

Below is the romance tropes list, ranked by demand signals. Each trope shows three counted numbers: demand signals (how many signals mention the trope as a main or secondary tag), tagged underserved or novel (the share of those signals that Tropesmith's classifier tagged that way, judged from each text alone) and from BookTok (how many of those signals came from BookTok comments and captions).

How to read this

A high demand-signal count means readers mention a trope often in reviews, comments and posts, whether asking for it or praising it. The tag is judged from each review or post alone, with no view of what is on the shelf, so it points at where to look. It does not prove that no book answers the signal. To see what already sits on the shelf, check the comp titles for your trope stack or look at a sample Map.

Why romance tropes matter for authors

Readers browse, shelve and buy by trope. A book that names the tropes readers mention — in its blurb, keywords and cover — meets readers where they are already searching. But popularity alone can mislead: a trope many readers mention is also a trope many authors write. Counting demand signals tells you how often readers mention a trope; the comp titles for your stack show how crowded its shelf already is. For the method behind it, see how to read reader demand signals and how to research tropes before you write.

Of the 16 romance tropes ranked on this page, Found Family has the most demand signals (300,602), followed by Slow Burn (125,290) and Forced Proximity (125,150). A demand signal is one specific thing a reader asked for or praised in a review, comment or post; counted 2026-10-07 17:24 UTC.

What is a romance trope?

A romance trope is a recurring, recognisable pattern readers actively seek out — a relationship setup, dynamic or premise like enemies-to-lovers, grumpy-sunshine or fated mates. Readers browse and buy by trope, which is why matching the tropes in demand is one of the strongest levers an author has.
