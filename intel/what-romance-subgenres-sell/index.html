What romance subgenres sell — and how to tell for yourself

Updated 13 August 2026 · ~7 min read

Ask “what romance subgenres sell best?” and you'll get a different answer every month — because the honest answer changes every month. Contemporary romance is the genre's centre of gravity, romantasy and dark romance have been the loudest BookTok stories of recent years, and cozy fantasy arrived almost overnight. But none of that tells you what matters: whether the lane you're considering can support the book you want to write, right now.

So this guide won't hand you a ranked league table that would be stale before you finished your draft. It teaches you the five signals that reveal whether a subgenre is selling — and shows you where to read each one live, for free.

The wrong question and the right one

“Which subgenre sells best?” is the wrong question, because the biggest lanes are also the most crowded. A huge market you can't get noticed in is worth less to you than a mid-sized market with visible gaps. The right question is: in which lane can my next book find readers it can actually win? That's a function of demand and supply, read together.

Five signals that tell you if a lane is selling

Every Tropesmith lane snapshot reports these live. Here's what each one means and how to read it:

SIGNAL 1

New chart entrants

How many fresh titles broke into the subgenre's bestseller charts in the recent window. This is the single clearest sign of a lane in motion: new books entering charts means readers are buying new authors, not just backlist giants. A lane where nothing new charts is a lane where the incumbents own the shelf.

SIGNAL 2

Surging titles and climbers

Books moving up the charts right now. Velocity matters more than position: a lane full of climbers is absorbing new demand, while a static top-100 suggests the same readers buying the same authors.

SIGNAL 3

Competing titles

The raw size of the field. High supply isn't automatically bad — big shelves mean big readerships — but read it against demand. A lane with heavy supply and thin chart movement is saturated. A lane with modest supply and busy charts is open.

SIGNAL 4

Typical price and Kindle Unlimited share

Pricing tells you the economics readers accept in that lane, and KU share tells you where its readers actually read. These two shape your earnings model and your distribution choice more than any craft decision will — we cover the KU decision fully in Kindle Unlimited vs wide.

SIGNAL 5

The rising trope

What's gaining pull inside the lane. Subgenres aren't monoliths: a flat-looking lane can hide a trope that's surging inside it, and that's often where the opportunity really lives. See how to research tropes before writing for the full method.

How to compare two lanes

Say you're torn between two subgenres you'd be equally happy writing. Open both snapshots side by side — for example Dark Romance and Romantic Suspense, or Cozy Fantasy and Romantasy — and ask three questions:

  • Which lane's charts are moving? More entrants and climbers means more room for a newcomer.
  • Which lane's economics fit your plan? A lane priced low and living in KU rewards series and rapid release; a higher-priced lane can support standalones.
  • Which lane's rising trope do you actually want to write? Demand you can't deliver with enthusiasm isn't your demand.

Two lanes can both be “selling” and still be completely different businesses. The snapshot numbers make the difference visible before you commit.

The qualitative layer: crowded vs open

Numbers first, but don't stop there. Scroll the actual bestseller shelf for the lane and look at the covers and blurbs. If every cover makes the same promise, the lane has converged on a formula — which is comforting for packaging but means differentiation happens inside narrow conventions. If the shelf looks scattered, reader expectations are looser and a strong, clear promise can stand out fast. Then read reader reviews for the phrase every market researcher loves: “I wish there were more books like…” That sentence is a gap wearing a label. When to check, and how often

Check when you're choosing the next book — not weekly while drafting, which is how authors end up chasing every twitch of the market. A lane rarely transforms inside one drafting cycle, but it does drift across a year. Frequently asked questions

What is the best-selling romance subgenre?

It changes as the market moves, which is why a static answer would mislead you. Contemporary romance is the genre's long-standing centre of gravity, while lanes like romantasy and dark romance have surged with BookTok. Check a live lane snapshot at tropesmith.com/market for what any subgenre is doing right now.

How do I know if a romance subgenre is oversaturated?

Read supply against demand: a lane with a large number of competing titles but few new chart entrants or climbers is saturated — incumbents own the shelf. A lane where new titles keep breaking into the charts is still absorbing new authors.

Should I write to a trending subgenre?

Only if you can write it with genuine enthusiasm and reach the market while demand is still ahead of supply. A trend you'd resent writing, or one already flooded by faster authors, is worse than a stable lane you love. Compare lanes on chart movement, economics, and whether you want to write what's rising.
