Publisher Rocket vs KDSpy

Updated 21 August 2026 · Written by Tropesmith, which competes with the tool on this page — read it with that in mind · Rival details: publisherrocket.com and kdspy.com

The short answer: Publisher Rocket is the deeper desktop tool — keyword research with competition scoring, category research, and Amazon Ads keyword lists. KDSpy is a browser extension built for speed: open an Amazon bestseller page, click, get an instant read on the niche. If you research thoroughly once per book, Rocket. If you triage many categories quickly, KDSpy. Both are typically one-time purchases rather than subscriptions; check each vendor for current pricing, which we do not track. And both describe the shelf that already exists — which is the thing neither is built to see past.

Rocket: depth

Rocket's advantage is that it scores competition behind a keyword rather than just listing it, finds categories at a granularity Amazon's own browse tree hides, and exports keyword lists straight into an ads workflow. It rewards sitting down with it for an hour.

KDSpy: speed

KDSpy's advantage is that it lives where you already are. You are looking at an Amazon category page anyway; one click turns it into estimated sales, review counts and rank spread. It rewards scanning twenty niches in the time Rocket would take on three.

What both share, and what both miss

Both read Amazon. Both describe supply: books that exist, keywords people already type, categories already populated. Neither carries any signal about demand that has not been met, because unmet demand has no book on the chart and no established search phrase. That is not a defect — it is outside the design of both — but it is the gap authors feel when the research is done and the question “so what do I actually write?” is still open.

What Tropesmith does instead

Here is the distinction that matters, and it is not a slight on anyone's tool. Keyword and category research answers how do I get this book found. It assumes the book exists. Tropesmith answers a question that comes earlier and that no keyword tool attempts: which story do readers keep asking for and not getting. We count individual reader requests — a real person, in a review or a comment or a BookTok video, describing a book they want and cannot find — classify each one by trope, and score it against what the shelf actually supplies in that lane. Tropesmith reads a registry of 310,000+ books, 3,140,000+ Goodreads reviews, 414,000+ book–trope tags across 49,900+ fully tagged titles, and 856,032 classified reader requests, scored across 138 demand lanes — plus an hourly scan of 620+ BookTok hashtags (95,900+ videos logged, 34,250 reader requests read from BookTok).

What Tropesmith is not. It is not a keyword tool: it will not give you Amazon search volumes or seven backend keyword strings. It does not estimate anyone's sales or revenue. It does not build or bid your ads. If those are the jobs in front of you, the tool on this page does them and Tropesmith does not replace it. Plenty of authors run both, and that is the honest recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Publisher Rocket or KDSpy - which should I buy?

Rocket if you do thorough research once per book and want keyword competition scoring and ads keyword lists. KDSpy if you scan many niches quickly and want a one-click read on an Amazon page you are already looking at. They overlap enough that most authors do not need both, and both are typically one-time purchases - check the vendors for current pricing.

Is KDSpy as accurate as Publisher Rocket?

On sales figures, both are estimating from Amazon rank, so both carry the same underlying uncertainty and neither should be treated as accounting. Rocket does more work on keyword competition, which is where the depth difference actually shows.

Do I need either of them?

If you publish on Amazon and have not done keyword and category research, one of them saves real hours. Neither, however, answers what to write - they describe the shelf that exists, not the requests it has failed to satisfy.

What do Publisher Rocket and KDSpy both miss?

Unmet demand. Both read Amazon's existing books, keywords and charts. A premise readers keep asking for and nobody has published leaves no trace there. Tropesmith counts those requests directly - 856,032 of them, classified by trope across 138 demand lanes.

Can I use a keyword tool and Tropesmith together?

That is the normal setup, and the one we would suggest. Tropesmith for the decision about what to write and which trope stack to build it on; Rocket or KDSpy for the keywords and categories once the book exists.
