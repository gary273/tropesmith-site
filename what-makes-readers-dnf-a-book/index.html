Rejection figures computed 27 September 2026 · Source: Tropesmith rejection-mining engine (1–2★ Goodreads reviews)

The pattern underneath the list

The top of this list isn't content-specific — it's craft-specific. Pacing, characterization and plot mechanics outrank any single trigger or content concern. Readers are more forgiving of a premise they don't love than of a premise they do love, badly executed.

Frequently asked questions

What's the single most common reason readers DNF a book?

By total mentions across the subgenres Tropesmith mines, "Slow / dragging pace" leads — flagged across 47 lanes with 79,718 total mentions in low-star reviews, an average 65% DNF rate where it's named.

How is this measured?

Tropesmith mines 1-2 star Goodreads reviews per subgenre for a fixed set of dealbreaker patterns (pacing, characterization, plot mechanics, content concerns), then tracks how often each pattern is named and what share of those reviews say the reader stopped reading entirely (DNF) versus merely disliked the book.

What is the most-named dealbreaker in each genre?

These are the most-named patterns in each genre, with mentions summed over the genre's lanes. in romance, "Inconsistent characterization" (39,477 mentions summed over the genre's lanes, 68% DNF rate); in mystery, "Slow / dragging pace" (10,435 mentions summed over the genre's lanes, 61% DNF rate); in fantasy, "Slow / dragging pace" (8,302 mentions summed over the genre's lanes, 67% DNF rate).
