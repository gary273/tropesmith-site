The pattern underneath the list

The top of this list isn't content-specific — it's craft-specific. Pacing, characterization and plot mechanics outrank any single trigger or content concern. Readers are more forgiving of a premise they don't love than of a premise they do love, badly executed.

Frequently asked questions

What's the single most common reason readers DNF a book?

By total mentions across the subgenres Tropesmith mines, "Slow / dragging pace" leads — flagged across 47 different subgenres with 65,672 total mentions in low-star reviews, an average 66% DNF rate where it's named.

How is this measured?

Tropesmith mines 1-2 star Goodreads reviews per subgenre for a fixed set of dealbreaker patterns (pacing, characterization, plot mechanics, content concerns), then tracks how often each pattern is named and what share of those reviews say the reader stopped reading entirely (DNF) versus merely disliked the book.

Is the top DNF reason the same in every genre?

No — it shifts by lane. in romance, the leading dealbreaker mentioned is "Ai-Generated" (21 mentions in Contemporary, 100% DNF rate); in mystery, the leading dealbreaker mentioned is "Poor Writing Quality" (16 mentions in Female Detectives & Sleuths, 100% DNF rate); in fantasy, the leading dealbreaker mentioned is "Unlikeable Characters" (16 mentions in Urban Fantasy, 100% DNF rate). The cross-genre table above shows the pattern that holds across all of them; these are the ones specific to a single lane.
