What "Found Family" promises readers

Found family gathers characters who choose each other as kin — a crew, a coven, a ragtag household — when blood relatives are absent, hostile, or simply beside the point. The emotional engine isn't the couple alone; it's the whole unit closing ranks around them, which is why found-family books so often run as series: the family has to be worth staying with book after book.

It's the single largest trope in Tropesmith's demand corpus by a wide margin, and it cuts across nearly every romance subgenre — cozy, dark, romantasy, sports — because "people who choose you" is a promise readers want regardless of setting. The 94% underserved figure doesn't mean nobody writes it; it means the specific found-family configurations readers are asking for (a found family that includes the villain's former crew, a found family built across a slow-burn ensemble cast) outrun what gets published in any given month.

How to use this before you draft

Read the three numbers above together, not separately. 267,433 requests tells you the trope is wanted at real scale; 94% underserved tells you how much of that want has no matching tagged supply yet; and the 11% recent-request share tells you whether that gap is closing or holding steady right now. A high request count with low underserved means the trope is proven but the shelf has largely caught up — the opportunity is in a specific unclaimed angle, not the trope's bare existence. A high underserved figure means more room, but pair it with the rejection data above: readers punish weak execution of a trope they want just as fast as they ignore one they don't.
